# Accident and Breakdown Reporting Procedure

Document code: ABR
Issued by: Safety and Operations, Sahyadri Freight Carriers Private Limited, Gut No.
217/3, Village Kalher, Bhiwandi–Kalyan Road, Bhiwandi, Dist. Thane 421302.
Applicability: All drivers, all depot managers and the Control Room, in respect of every
accident, breakdown or spill involving a Company vehicle (BWD, PNU, NSK, VPI).

This Procedure governs the reporting of accidents and breakdowns, the securing of the
scene, the intimation of the insurer, and the recovery of a disabled vehicle. It is to be
read with the Driver Allowance and Overnight Halt Policy (DAP), the Fuel Card and
Reimbursement Standard Operating Procedure (FCR) and the Preventive Maintenance Schedule
(PMS).

## 1. Definitions

1.1 In this Procedure, unless the context otherwise requires —

(a) "Incident" means any accident, breakdown, fire, theft or spill involving a Company
vehicle, whether or not any third party or injury is involved.

(b) "Reportable Accident" means an Incident involving death of or bodily injury to any
person, or damage to the property of a third party, or damage to the Company vehicle.

(c) "Control Room" means the 24-hour operations desk at the Bhiwandi Central Hub (BWD)
that receives first intimation of an Incident and initiates the response.

(d) "First Intimation" means the driver's earliest telephonic report of an Incident to
the Control Room.

(e) "Removal" means the protection of a disabled vehicle and its towing to the nearest
repairer, and its re-delivery to the Company.

(f) "Cordon" means the area cleared and kept clear around a spill of a dangerous or
hazardous substance.

(g) "Applicable Policy" means, in relation to a vehicle, the package policy of insurance
covering that vehicle's class under Section 2 of this Procedure.

(h) "Total Loss" means a loss where the vehicle is treated as a constructive total loss
under the Applicable Policy, the threshold for which is fixed in that policy and applied,
for maintenance purposes, through PMS-7.1 of the Preventive Maintenance Schedule.

## 2. Insurance mapping and its origin

2.1 The class of a vehicle determines the Applicable Policy, as follows.

| Class | Applicable Policy | Insurer | Owner-driver personal-accident cover |
|-------|-------------------|---------|--------------------------------------|
| A | Goods Carrying Vehicle Package Policy | Raheja QBE General Insurance | as fixed under Section IV of that policy |
| B | Goods Carrying Vehicle Package Policy | Raheja QBE General Insurance | as fixed under Section IV of that policy |
| C | Commercial Vehicle Package Policy | Magma HDI General Insurance | as fixed under Section IV of that policy |
| D | Commercial Vehicle Package Policy | Magma HDI General Insurance | as fixed under Section IV of that policy |

2.2 The split in ABR-2.1 is a legacy of history rather than a matter of preference. The
Class A and Class B fleet has from the outset been insured under the Raheja QBE Goods
Carrying Vehicle Package Policy. The Class C and Class D fleet came to the Company through
the acquisition of an earlier operator whose heavy vehicles already stood on the Magma HDI
Commercial Vehicle Package Policy, and those cover lines were carried over at renewal.

2.3 In consequence of ABR-2.2, the Class C and Class D vehicles carry Endorsement IMT-23
on the Magma HDI Commercial Vehicle Package Policy, while the Class A and Class B vehicles
do not; a depot manager settling a Class C or Class D own-damage matter shall have regard
to that endorsement, and shall not assume it for a Class A or Class B vehicle.

2.4 The precise sums and limits under the Applicable Policy, including the owner-driver
personal-accident cover under Section IV and the Removal indemnity under Section I(3), are
not reproduced in this Procedure; they are to be read from the policy wording itself for
the class in question, this Procedure supplying only the mapping of class to policy.

## 3. Immediate response and reporting

3.1 On any Incident, the driver shall make First Intimation to the Control Room within
**35 minutes** of the Incident, stating the location, the vehicle, and whether any person
is injured. This is the earliest step and is not displaced by any other obligation in this
Procedure.

3.2 The driver shall attend to any injured person and shall not move the vehicle from the
position in which it comes to rest except so far as is necessary for safety, in discharge
of the duty of a driver in the case of an accident under Section 134 of the Motor Vehicles
Act, 1988. Where the Incident is a Reportable Accident, the driver shall also report to
the nearest police station.

3.3 The driver shall submit a written incident report in Form ABR/IR-1, with such
photographs and particulars as the form requires, within **22 hours** of the Incident. The
written report supplements and does not replace the First Intimation under ABR-3.1.

3.4 The Control Room shall open an Incident record on First Intimation and shall note
against it the load condition of the vehicle, drawing where necessary on the trip record
maintained under FCR-5.2 of the Fuel Card and Reimbursement Standard Operating Procedure,
so that any question of overloading at the material time is on record.

## 4. Intimation of the insurer

4.1 The Control Room shall intimate the insurer under the Applicable Policy for the class
of vehicle, identified from ABR-2.1, and shall quote the policy in the intimation.

4.2 Intimation to the insurer shall be made within **51 hours** of the Incident:
Provided that where the Incident is a hit-and-run case or involves the death of a third
party, the matter shall be intimated within the **35-minute** First Intimation window
under ABR-3.1 and shall not wait upon the 51-hour period, which in such cases does not
apply.

4.3 A claim for own damage to a Class C or Class D vehicle shall be presented with
reference to Endorsement IMT-23 where the damage falls within it; a like claim for a Class
A or Class B vehicle shall be presented under the Goods Carrying Vehicle Package Policy
without reference to that endorsement.

4.4 The personal-accident entitlement of an owner-driver arising from the Incident is that
fixed under Section IV of the Applicable Policy for the class, and the Control Room shall
resolve the sum by reading that Section of the policy wording for the class concerned and
not from this Procedure.

## 5. Breakdown and recovery

5.1 On a breakdown, the driver shall secure the vehicle and its load, place warning
devices, and make First Intimation under ABR-3.1 as for any other Incident.

5.2 Removal to the nearest repairer shall be arranged through the Control Room. The cost
of protection and Removal is met in the first instance out of the Removal indemnity under
Section I(3) of the Applicable Policy for the class of vehicle.

5.3 Where the cost of protection and Removal exceeds the Removal indemnity available under
Section I(3) of the Applicable Policy, the excess only may be met from the depot breakdown
recovery float of **Rs. 6,300** per Incident. The float does not duplicate the policy
indemnity; it meets the shortfall above it, and the driver shall route any amount he has
paid out of pocket through this clause and not as an ordinary fuel claim, consistent with
FCR-6.3 of the Fuel Card and Reimbursement Standard Operating Procedure.

5.4 A vehicle recovered under this Section is taken into the shop and dealt with under the
Preventive Maintenance Schedule; it is not released for dispatch until PMS-7 of that
Schedule is satisfied.

## 6. Spill of dangerous or hazardous goods

6.1 On a spill of a dangerous or hazardous substance, the driver shall make First
Intimation under ABR-3.1, shall report to the nearest police station as required in
respect of such carriage, and shall keep the public away from the spill.

6.2 The driver shall establish a Cordon of not less than **55 m** radius around the spill
and shall not permit any ignition source within it, until the Control Room or the
authorities take charge.

6.3 A spill shall be escalated on the timetable in ABR-7 and shall be treated as a
Reportable Accident for the purposes of insurer intimation under ABR-4.

## 7. Escalation timetable

7.1 An Incident is escalated on the following time-ordered basis, measured from the time
of the Incident.

| Window | Action |
|--------|--------|
| 0 – 1 hour | First Intimation to Control Room under ABR-3.1 (within 35 minutes); injured attended; scene secured; police informed in a Reportable Accident |
| 1 – 24 hours | Written report in Form ABR/IR-1 under ABR-3.3 (within 22 hours); Incident record completed; load condition noted |
| 24 – 72 hours | Insurer intimated under ABR-4.2 (within 51 hours), save for the cases in the proviso to ABR-4.2; Removal and survey progressed |

## 8. Records and audit

8.1 The Incident record, Form ABR/IR-1, the insurer intimation and the Removal papers
shall be retained for not less than 7 years and produced on demand.

8.2 A driver who fails to make First Intimation within the window in ABR-3.1, or who moves
a vehicle in breach of ABR-3.2, shall be referred to Safety and his safety score reviewed.
