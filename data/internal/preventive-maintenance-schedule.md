# Preventive Maintenance Schedule

Document code: PMS
Issued by: Engineering and Operations, Sahyadri Freight Carriers Private Limited, Gut No.
217/3, Village Kalher, Bhiwandi–Kalyan Road, Bhiwandi, Dist. Thane 421302.
Applicability: All vehicles on the rolls of the Company, all depot managers and the
workshop at each depot (BWD, PNU, NSK, VPI).

This Schedule governs the intervals at which a vehicle is serviced, the pre-monsoon
inspection, the wear limits at which components are to be replaced, and the withdrawal and
condemnation of a vehicle. It is to be read with the Driver Allowance and Overnight Halt
Policy (DAP), the Fuel Card and Reimbursement Standard Operating Procedure (FCR) and the
Accident and Breakdown Reporting Procedure (ABR).

## 1. Definitions

1.1 In this Schedule, unless the context otherwise requires —

(a) "Service" means a scheduled preventive maintenance attention of a defined type,
carried out at the workshop against a checklist for the vehicle class.

(b) "Service Interval" means the distance in kilometres, or the running in engine-hours,
whichever falls first, after which a Service of a given type becomes due.

(c) "Engine-hour" means one hour of engine running as recorded by the hour-meter, used
for the heavy classes where distance alone understates duty.

(d) "Since-service Distance" means the distance run by a vehicle since its last recorded
Service, computed as the current odometer less the odometer at last Service.

(e) "Roadworthiness" means the condition of a vehicle such that it complies with the
construction, equipment and maintenance requirements applicable to it, including a
subsisting certificate of fitness.

(f) "Certificate of Fitness" means the certificate of fitness required for a transport
vehicle, the validity of which is governed by Rule 62 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules,
1989.

(g) "Condemnation" means the permanent withdrawal of a vehicle from service, whether on
grounds of its condition or because it is a constructive total loss under the applicable
package policy.

(h) "Pre-monsoon Inspection" means the additional inspection carried out once a year,
before the onset of the monsoon, in accordance with PMS-5.

## 2. Scope

2.1 Every vehicle is serviced on the Service Interval fixed for its class in PMS-4.1,
and is additionally subject to the Pre-monsoon Inspection under PMS-5 and to the wear
limits in PMS-6.

2.2 A vehicle that is due for a Service, or that fails a wear limit in PMS-6, is withdrawn
from service and its Fuel Card is de-activated under FCR-3.1 of the Fuel Card and
Reimbursement Standard Operating Procedure; a driver shall not be rostered onto it under
DAP-6.1 of the Driver Allowance and Overnight Halt Policy until it is released.

## 3. Responsibility

3.1 The depot workshop is responsible for carrying out each Service, recording the
odometer and engine-hours at Service, and updating the vehicle record.

3.2 The Depot Manager is responsible for withdrawing a due or unfit vehicle from dispatch
and for the correctness of the Since-service Distance on the record.

## 4. Service intervals

4.1 A vehicle becomes due for a Service of the stated type on reaching the distance or, for
the heavy classes, the engine-hours shown below for its class, whichever falls first.

| Service type | Class A | Class B | Class C | Class D |
|--------------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| S1 — Running inspection and oil check (every) | 14,200 km | 12,300 km | 11,500 km / 480 eng-hr | 9,800 km / 420 eng-hr |
| S2 — Major service (every third S1) | 42,600 km | 36,900 km | 34,500 km / 1,440 eng-hr | 29,400 km / 1,260 eng-hr |

4.2 The S1 interval is the governing interval for the class; the S2 interval is three
times the S1 interval and a Service is not deferred beyond whichever of distance or
engine-hours falls first.

4.3 A Class C vehicle whose Since-service Distance exceeds 11,500 km, or a Class D vehicle
whose Since-service Distance exceeds 9,800 km, is overdue and shall be withdrawn from
dispatch until serviced, notwithstanding that its engine-hours may not yet have been
reached.

4.4 A Service falling due while a vehicle is on an R3 (trunk) trip under the Driver
Allowance and Overnight Halt Policy shall be carried out at the nearest depot workshop on
completion of the trip, and the trip shall not be extended so as to defer the Service.

## 5. Pre-monsoon inspection

5.1 Every vehicle shall undergo a Pre-monsoon Inspection once each year in the window
**15 May to 9 June**, in addition to its scheduled Service.

5.2 The Pre-monsoon Inspection shall cover, at least, the condition of tyres, brakes,
wipers, lights, the sealing of the cab and load body, the battery and the underbody, so
that the vehicle is fit for monsoon running.

5.3 A vehicle that fails the Pre-monsoon Inspection is withdrawn from service and dealt
with under PMS-6 and PMS-7 as the failure requires.

## 6. Wear limits

6.1 A component that has reached its wear limit shall be replaced at the Service then due,
and shall not be run to failure. The governing wear limits are as follows.

| Component | Internal condemnation limit | Regulatory reference |
|-----------|-----------------------------|----------------------|
| Brake lining residual thickness | not less than 3.2 mm | Rule 96, Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 (brakes) |
| Tyre non-skid depth | not less than 2.4 mm | Rule 94, Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 (statutory floor 1.6 mm) |

6.2 The residual thickness of a brake lining shall not be less than **3.2 mm**; a lining
at or below that thickness shall be replaced, the brakes being maintained to the standard
required under Rule 96 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989.

6.3 The Company fixes its own tyre condemnation limit at a non-skid depth of **2.4 mm**.
This internal limit is set above the statutory minimum non-skid depth of 1.6 mm required
under Rule 94 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989; a tyre at or below 2.4 mm non-skid
depth is condemned and replaced even though it may still meet the statutory 1.6 mm figure,
and the higher internal limit governs.

6.4 The emission of the vehicle shall be kept within the limits under Rule 115 of the
Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989, and a subsisting certificate under the smoke and
carbon-monoxide test under Rule 116 of those Rules shall be held at all times.

## 7. Condemnation and total loss

7.1 Where the estimated cost of repair of a vehicle reaches **62% of its Insured Declared
Value**, the vehicle shall be placed before a surveyor for a condemnation decision. That
internal survey trigger sits below the constructive-total-loss line in the applicable
package policy, under which the vehicle is a constructive total loss only where the
aggregate cost of retrieval and repair exceeds 75% of the Insured Declared Value; the
internal trigger initiates the survey, and the policy line determines the loss.

7.2 On completion of a Service the vehicle is released to available status and cleared for
dispatch: Provided that a vehicle whose Certificate of Fitness under Rule 62 of the
Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 has lapsed shall remain in the shop and shall not be
released, notwithstanding that its Service stands completed, until the certificate is
renewed.

7.3 A vehicle taken into the shop following an Incident under ABR-5.4 of the Accident and
Breakdown Reporting Procedure shall be assessed under PMS-7.1 before any repair is
authorised, so that a constructive total loss is identified before, and not after, money
is spent on it.

## 8. Records and audit

8.1 The Service record, the Pre-monsoon Inspection record, the wear-limit measurements and
any condemnation survey shall be retained for not less than 7 years and produced on demand
to an inspecting officer.

8.2 A vehicle found to have been dispatched while overdue under PMS-4.3, or while unfit
under PMS-6 or PMS-7.2, shall be reported to Engineering and the lapse recorded against the
depot.
